---
title: How to use this workbook
---

This workbook is Volume II of the course. Volume I, the theory book, teaches each day's ideas; this volume is where you use them, by hand and against a clock. Both volumes have the same 29 chapters, and each chapter number is a day of the 29-day plan, from Saturday 7 November to Saturday 5 December 2026. Write in it. A workbook that stays clean has not been used.

## A day in the workbook {.unnumbered}

Every chapter has the same parts, in the order you meet them during the day.

1. **Today's plan.** The day's blocks with their times, the plan's notes for the day and why the day matters for Infosys. Write your start time, end time, energy and sleep in the line below the notes; over four weeks they show when you work best.
2. **Spaced review.** The patterns taught 1, 3, 7 and 14 days ago. Cover the theory book, write each template from memory, then compare it with the listing in the chapter shown and tick *Again*, *Shaky* or *Solid*. "Solid" means it was right the first time.
3. **Exercises.** Work them after reading the theory chapter and before the problems. They move from a complete example in the theory book to a faded one with gaps, and then to work of your own: fill the gaps, trace by hand, judge a statement, design a solution, find a bug, size a method from its limits, and write a template from memory.
4. **Problem worksheets.** One per problem of the day. Fill in the top rows *before* you type any code: the pattern, the target complexity from the largest limit (Theory Appendix A) and the key idea in one sentence.
5. **Re-solve from three days ago.** Pick one problem from three days earlier and solve it again without your old code.
6. **End of day.** The error log for the day and two lines of reflection.

On the five mock days (Chapters 9, 16, 23, 25 and 27) the problem worksheets are replaced by the mock sheet: the rules, a planning grid for the first fifteen minutes, a score sheet and a post-mortem. Do that chapter's exercises after the mock, not before.

## Time limits and hints {.unnumbered}

Each problem has a time limit: Easy 20 minutes, Medium 40, Hard 60. When it runs out, open the hint for that problem in Appendix A. If you are still stuck ten minutes later, read the editorial, close it, and write the solution yourself. Tick how the problem ended on its worksheet: solved alone, after the hint, after the editorial, or not yet. "Not yet" problems go into the error log.

Core problems come first. The bonus problems are the Specialist Programmer level; attempt them only if the core is done and you still feel sharp.

## Answers {.unnumbered}

Answers to every exercise are in Appendix B. Check them after you finish a whole exercise, not line by line while you work. Every number in them was produced by running code, so if your number differs, look for the mistake in your own work first, then compare the method. Where an exercise has no single right answer, such as writing a template from memory, the answer says what to check.

## The error log {.unnumbered}

Every mistake goes into the error log: the problem, its topic, the cause and a rule that would have prevented it. Use one of nine causes: misread statement, wrong idea, overflow or modulo, off-by-one, time limit, input reading, edge case, stack overflow, ran out of time. Write the rule so that you can check it against code in ten seconds ("cast the first factor to `long`"), not as an intention ("be careful"). Each entry gets a re-solve date three days later. Each chapter ends with a small log, and Appendix C has full pages for the rest. The repair session of Chapter 26 starts from this log.

## The trackers {.unnumbered}

Two trackers follow this page. The **progress tracker** has a box for every problem of the plan: tick it when the problem is solved, alone or after a hint. Shaded boxes are bonus problems. The **pattern recall tracker** lists all 44 handbook patterns with the days on which each comes back for review. Write A, S or G (again, shaky, solid) in each cell after the review. Any pattern still at A on Day 26 goes into that day's repair session.

Appendix D collects the scores of the five mocks on one page, next to the checkpoints that say whether you are on track. Appendix E is squared paper for anything that does not fit elsewhere.

## Stop before you burn out {.unnumbered}

The plan's signs that you are tired are two problems in a row past their time limit, reading the same line of a statement twice, and pasting code you do not understand. When you notice them, stop starting new problems, do the review block and end the day. Skip the bonus hour freely: the core problems alone cover every pattern. Sleep at least seven hours, especially before a mock.
