01 · The Thesis

Why this works, in three levers

Your salary isn't low because your skills are weak. It's low because of where those skills are parked — and the Indian market in late 2026 is paying unusually well for exactly the move you can make.

01
The services-to-product arbitrage
The same title pays radically differently depending on who signs the cheque. Reported 2026 benchmarks put a Java developer with five years at a services firm around ₹8–12L, and the same five years at a product company or global capability centre at ₹18–25L. At senior level the spread widens to roughly ₹18–24L versus ₹35–50L. Nothing about the engineer changes; only the employer's business model does.
Worth 2–3× on its own
02
The AI premium — at two levels
Mid-2026 Indian salary data shows professionals with practical AI-tooling competence commanding roughly a 25–40% premium over peers who lack it, and one Indian study puts AI roles 38% above traditional IT baselines. Demand is still accelerating: Naukri's JobSpeak shows AI/ML hiring up 31% year on year in August 2026, against 14% for white-collar hiring overall. The premium pays at two levels — using AI tools well, which everyone must now do, and building AI systems, which is rarer and better paid. This plan trains both.
Worth a further 25–40%
03
The Java-AI gap
Most people chasing the AI premium do it in Python, while a large share of the enterprise systems that AI has to plug into run on the JVM. The Java side has now matured: Spring AI 2.0 went GA on 12 June 2026, designed for Spring Boot 4.0 and 4.1, and building an MCP server with it is largely a matter of annotating a Spring bean. You already have deep Spring muscle. You don't need to abandon your stack to enter the best-paid niche — you need to point it there.
Makes you rare instead of one of many
The single sentence version

For 90 days you become "the Spring Boot engineer who has shipped a production RAG and agent service — with evaluation, cost metering, observability and an MCP interface", and you sell that to product companies and GCCs instead of to a services firm. That sentence is still rare enough in India to be worth several times what you're paid now.

Four things I'm going to be honest about

Honesty 1 · The GCC experience wall is real

An analysis of more than 5,000 live GCC openings and 20,000 GCC hires found about 62% of hiring concentrated at three to eight years of experience, with a median around six. You have roughly one year, so an "apply only to GCCs" strategy would mostly bounce.

The plan therefore spreads your effort across four employer types — product companies, AI-first startups, the genuinely junior GCC requisitions, and a local fallback — weighted toward the ones that hire on demonstrated capability rather than years served. That's why so much of the plan builds visible proof instead of certificates.

Honesty 2 · ₹12–18L is the 90-day target, not ₹40L

The ₹35–50L figures attach to senior engineers: they're your 2029 number, not your December number. Anyone promising ₹40L from one year of experience plus 90 days of study is selling something.

What 90 focused days can genuinely produce is a written offer in the ₹10–18L range, with a shot at the low ₹20s if two offers collide. For calibration, Glassdoor's 2026 data puts average SDE-1 pay in Bengaluru near ₹14.4L, with the 90th percentile near ₹23.9L. That's a 3×–5× jump for you — and it makes ₹35L+ reachable in two more moves, because each raise compounds off the new base, not off ₹3.4L.

Honesty 3 · Location is a decision you make in week one

The premiums concentrate in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Gurgaon and Chennai; JobSpeak attributes August's GCC hiring growth mainly to southern cities. Ahmedabad and GIFT City are within reach of Nadiad but have a lower ceiling, and fully remote roles at this band are the most contested segment in the market. You don't have to want to relocate — but you must decide, on Day 4, what you'll accept, because every recruiter asks in the first three minutes.

Honesty 4 · Your employer's clock and your notice period

Two constraints v1 ignored. First, TCS's deployment policy, as reported: associates must be allocated for at least 225 business days in any 12 months, which effectively caps bench time at about 35 business days, and non-compliance can affect pay, promotion, overseas postings and continuity of employment. A March 2026 report said the cap still applies, with evaluations starting after it and unallocated staff possibly asked to exit. Counting from your 9 October release, that allowance could run out around the end of November — before most offers from a September start will have landed.

Second, your notice period is widely reported by TCS employees as 90 days, while a March 2026 report found 58% of employers want immediate joiners. The plan handles both: internal allocation becomes the first job on release day, your bench clock is tracked weekly, and a notice script, early-release request and resign-on-signing rule are built in. Confirm your own numbers in writing on Day 16 — policies are applied case by case, and your offer letter governs.

The refusal you got was not a verdict on your worth. It was a price quote from one buyer. This plan is about getting other quotes — without losing the job you have while you do it.

How to apply — the part that decides your response rate
  • Be findable where recruiters search. Naukri remains where many Indian in-house recruiters source; complete it to 100% and refresh it weekly. Add Instahyre and Cutshort, which do curated tech matching, and Wellfound for startups. Sourced candidates are reported to be several times more likely to be hired than people who apply cold.
  • Never apply alone. Cold applications draw roughly 2–3% responses in 2026. Pair every Core and Dream application with one human — a referral request, a recruiter message or a hiring-manager note.
  • Mirror the job description. Pull the phrases that recur across 20 target JDs — "Spring Boot", "microservices", "Kafka", "Kubernetes", "REST APIs", "AWS" — and use them naturally. Screening is keyword-first, human-second.
  • Apply to roles marked two to four years. Experience ranges are filters, not laws, and strong proof routinely beats a year of tenure. Don't reject yourself on the company's behalf.
  • Check who actually employs you. Contract engagements in India reportedly grew about 46% from 2025 to 2026 while permanent hiring dipped. "On the rolls of" a staffing firm, or contract-to-hire, is not the same offer as a permanent role at the same headline.
  • Never pay anyone. No legitimate employer charges for applications, interviews, training, "security deposits" or offer letters. On 22 September 2026 the Home Affairs ministry flagged a surge in fake overseas job offers built on exactly these fees.

07 · The Gauntlet

Every round, and what it's really testing

Backend interview loops in India are predictable — and in 2026 two new rounds have joined them: software that screens you before a human does, and coding rounds where AI is allowed. Knowing what each round actually measures is worth more than another fifty problems.

Round 0 · newMachine screens — résumé parsers and AI interviewsBefore any human

Résumés are parsed and ranked by keyword match, and AI-conducted screening interviews — recorded answers scored automatically — are spreading. One widely cited figure puts automated video screening at about one company in three; in an April 2026 survey, 68.5% of candidates said they were never told AI was involved; and roughly 70% of Indian HR and business leaders report using generative AI in some capacity, most heavily in recruiting.

What to do
  • Single-column résumé in the job description's own vocabulary (Section 09).
  • When an AI-interview link arrives, do it within 24 hours: quiet room, good light, camera at eye level, stable connection.
  • Answer the question in your first sentence, then give structure (situation, action, result), then stop. Automated scorers reward clarity, structure and directness.
  • Rehearsed on camera on Day 20.
Round 1Recruiter screen15–25 min · phone

Not technical. It filters for plausible experience and stack, a salary expectation inside the band, notice-period fit, and whether you can hold a conversation. More candidates die here than in any coding round — usually by fumbling the CTC question, the notice question, or sounding bitter about their current job.

Have these ready, word for word
  • A 45-second introduction that ends on your strongest proof, not your job title.
  • Expected CTC — ₹14L — said without apology and without a question mark.
  • Your notice answer: 90 days contractual, plus the early-release status you confirmed on Day 16 (script in Section 08).
  • Your location answer, decided on Day 4 — not "I'm flexible".
  • A forward-looking reason for leaving, rehearsed on Day 49.
Round 2Online assessment / DSA screen60–90 min

Two or three easy-to-medium problems, sometimes with Java and SQL multiple-choice questions. A pure throughput filter — the most mechanical round to prepare for, which is why the daily DSA slot starts on Day 1 instead of being crammed later.

Scope that is enough

The problems scheduled on this board, across the standard patterns: hashing, two pointers, sliding window, binary search, stacks, linked lists, trees, heaps, backtracking, graphs, intervals and dynamic programming. Patterns, not volume: solving 400 problems without naming the pattern you used is worse preparation than fewer problems with notes you wrote.

Round 3Core Java, Spring & SQL depth45–60 min · live

Your strongest round, and the one you can make decisive. Expect collections internals, equals and hashCode, immutability, concurrency, JVM memory and garbage collection, Spring IoC and bean lifecycle, transaction propagation, AOP — then SQL joins, indexing and query plans.

Where candidates fold, and you won't
  • Modern Java, not Java 8. Records, sealed types, pattern matching, virtual threads — and the 2026-current detail that since JDK 24 (JEP 491) a synchronized block no longer pins a virtual thread. You'll have measured it on Day 6.
  • Spring Boot 4, not just 3. API versioning, JSpecify null-safety, built-in retry, HTTP service clients, Jackson 3 — and what breaks in a 3-to-4 migration. Day 7.
  • Transaction propagation. What REQUIRES_NEW does inside a failing outer transaction — almost nobody answers cleanly. Day 40.
  • Query plans. EXPLAIN ANALYZE, index choice, and a real N+1 you found and killed. Day 34.
  • Concurrency beyond the textbook. A race you caused and fixed, executor sizing, scoped values versus ThreadLocal. Day 54.
Round 4Low-level design60 min · code or class diagram

Parking lot, elevator bank, rate limiter, notification service, Splitwise. They're testing whether you can turn a vague requirement into clean classes with sane responsibilities — the day job, in miniature. At one to three years this round is often weighted more heavily than system design.

The method

Clarify requirements aloud. Name the entities. Define interfaces before implementations. Apply one or two patterns deliberately and say why — Strategy for interchangeable pricing, Factory for creation, Observer for notifications. Then let them push on extension; good design absorbs "what if we add a second vehicle type?" without a rewrite.

Round 5High-level system design45–60 min · whiteboard

URL shortener, news feed, chat, distributed rate limiter, payments. At your level they want structured thinking and honest trade-offs, not buzzword bingo.

The six-step frame — every time
  1. Functional and non-functional requirements. Ask, don't assume.
  2. Back-of-envelope scale: users, QPS, storage, read/write ratio.
  3. API surface — concrete endpoints.
  4. Data model and storage choice, with a reason.
  5. High-level diagram: clients, gateway, services, cache, queue, database.
  6. Deep-dive wherever they push, and name the bottlenecks before they do.

Your unfair advantage: you've built and debugged a seven-service system, and by week six you'll have designed Lectern for real load. Speak from that, not from a video course — interviewers can tell instantly.

Round 6 · newAI-assisted coding round60 min · assistant provided

The newest round and the one candidates are least ready for. In CoderPad's 2026 survey, 46% of hiring leaders said they allow AI in technical interviews or assessments, broadly or with constraints, and another 20% decide case by case. Meta has piloted an AI-enabled round that replaces one onsite coding round, often inside a larger, multi-file codebase; Google, Canva and others have run versions of their own. Canva is described as expecting you to defend what the assistant produced.

What's graded — and the method
  • Decompose the problem aloud before you prompt.
  • Prompt with context, constraints and examples; ask for small pieces, not the whole solution.
  • Read every line. Say out loud when the assistant is wrong, and why.
  • Write or extend the tests yourself — verification is the skill being measured.
  • Never submit code you can't explain line by line.
  • Rehearsed on Day 20 (single problem) and Day 41 (unfamiliar multi-file codebase).

The ethical line: use AI only in rounds where the company explicitly allows it. Anywhere else it's cheating, it is increasingly detected, and some companies have been bringing final rounds back in person partly because of it.

Round 7Hiring manager & behavioural45 min

Decides your level, and therefore your number. One question in several disguises: will this person be a problem, and will they still be here in two years?

Your raw material is unusually strong — use it
  • Teaching five technologies solo to engineer batches on a compressed timeline. Frame it as depth and communication, never as "I wasn't doing real dev work".
  • Interviewing candidates on Spring Boot and Angular — you've been trusted to judge other engineers.
  • Shipping AnimaFlow and Lectern alone — ownership is what they're buying.
  • The pay negotiation itself, told without bitterness: a paper trail, deliberate escalation, leverage held in reserve, facts throughout.
  • "Are you on the bench?" — if you are, answer plainly and without defensiveness (script in Section 08). Evasion reads far worse than the fact.

Negotiating the written offerDay 73 · or whenever a letter arrives
"Thank you — I'm genuinely excited about this, and I want to say clearly that I intend to accept. One thing I'd like to work through. The fixed component is ₹[X]. THEN STOP TALKING. Let the silence sit. IF THEY IMPROVE IT: "That works. Please send the revised letter and I'll sign today." IF THEY HOLD FIRM: "Understood. Could you do [joining bonus / six-month review / support with my notice period]?" — take the secondary item, then accept, or decline cleanly if it's genuinely below your floor.
Mechanics that matter: negotiate only once a written offer exists. Enthusiasm first, then one number, one reason, and silence — filling your own silence is how candidates negotiate against themselves. Negotiate fixed pay: in-hand typically runs 20–30% below the CTC headline, and two identical-looking offers can differ widely in what reaches your account.

If you have no offer: you are not back where you started. You have two live systems, problems of pattern notes, a real network and a live pipeline. Run a 30-day extension on conversion alone — mocks and closing, nothing new.

Three protocols for moments you can't schedule

Offer Protocol
Run it whenever an offer lands — not on a date
  1. On the call: thank them, don't accept, ask for the written break-up, and promise an answer within 48 hours.
  2. Read it properly: fixed versus variable, the payroll entity (their own rolls, or a staffing firm or contract-to-hire), the joining date against your 90-day notice, and what BGV will require.
  3. Score it on the comparison sheet (Day 71).
  4. Negotiate once, with the script — fixed pay first.
  5. Tell your other live processes honestly, with the urgency script.
  6. Sign; resign the same or the next working day; request early release in that same email.
  7. Keep one warm backup until your joining date. Never accept two offers.
Notice Protocol
90 days is a constraint, not a secret
  • The facts: widely reported by TCS employees as 90 calendar days; early release is at management discretion; employers can recover notice pay for unserved days or hold back a relieving letter where the contract allows. Confirm your own terms on Day 16.
  • Say it early — in the first call, with the script.
  • Ask every company whether they'll wait, support an early-release request, or cover a buyout — and get the answer into the offer letter.
  • The resign-early gambit (resigning before an offer to shorten the wait): only if all three hold — at least three processes at final stages, recruiters explicitly naming your notice as the blocker, and at least four months of expenses saved (your Day 1 runway number). Otherwise, don't.
Bench Protocol
Protect the job while you replace it
  • The rule, as reported: 225 allocated business days in any 12 months — roughly 35 bench business days — then evaluations and a possible exit. Get your own count in writing on Day 16.
  • Allocation first. Accept any reasonable billable project; this plan runs in your evenings.
  • Every day: an RMG follow-up. Every week: a Bench Clock entry.
  • About 15 business days in (late October): escalate — message project managers again and apply to every internal opening.
  • About 25 business days in (mid-November): take any reasonable allocation you're offered, even a dull one.
  • If an exit conversation starts: stay calm and factual, sign nothing on the spot, and ask in writing for the separation type, your last working day, and your relieving and experience letters; ask for time to find a project. For anything contractual, get qualified advice.

Twelve hard rules

Break these and the plan stops working
  1. Never stop applying, not even while interviewing. The moment your pipeline empties you lose the ability to walk away, and that ability is your negotiating power.
  2. Never take a certification instead of shipping something. It feels like progress and costs weeks — the cancelled Wings T15 roadmap already did that to you once.
  3. Never reveal your current CTC before stating your expectation — and never misstate it. Deflect on the phone; never deceive on paper. BGV checks.
  4. Never accept an offer on the phone. "Thank you — please send the written break-up; I'll respond within 48 hours."
  5. Never apply to a Dream-tier company before Day 42. A rejection can close that door for months.
  6. Never pay anyone for a job — no registration, training, deposit or "processing" fee. Legitimate employers don't charge.
  7. Never put TCS material anywhere public or personal — not in Lectern's corpus, not on your drive, not in a screenshot.
  8. Never take live payments while employed without checking TCS's code of conduct on outside business activity. Stripe test mode demonstrates everything an interviewer needs.
  9. Never ship without a README that sells it — and never go 48 hours without a commit. Unexplained code is invisible, and the contribution graph is read as a proxy for consistency.
  10. Never badmouth TCS in an interview. Your network there is a twenty-year asset, and interviewers hear complaints as a preview.
  11. Never skip the back protocol. Documented disc changes at 22, plus hundreds of added hours of sitting.
  12. Never re-run the whole decision between checkpoints. Adjust one variable at a checkpoint, then get back to work.
What success looks like on Day 90

A signed offer between ₹10L and ₹18L. Lectern and AnimaFlow live, with READMEs a stranger can follow. problems solved with pattern notes you wrote. applications and personal messages behind you, and engineers at dozens of companies who know your name. Two or three posts published. A clean exit from TCS, references intact, with your record protected throughout.

None of it depends on luck, a visa, an exam or anyone's permission. It depends on opening this file every morning and doing the card in front of you.

You spent a year fighting for a raise inside a band whose ceiling was set before you arrived. The same effort, pointed outward for 90 days — while protecting the job you have — is worth several times more.
